# Construction and commissioning power: evidence and qualification

## Conclusion

Large projects can purchase relatively small temporary-power packages during
construction or commissioning. A Texas wind project provides a concrete
example close to the proposed 300 MW / 3 MW planning scenario. This supports
searching the market, not a universal one-percent sizing rule or an open order
for 2G. Parent capacity and temporary package capacity must remain separate.

The commercial target is a separately scoped temporary-power package bought
by an owner, EPC or commissioning contractor. Evidence was reviewed September
11, 2026. Completed case studies are not current opportunities.

## Application evidence

### Azure Sky, Texas

Enel identifies Azure Sky as an operating 350 MW wind project.[1] Aggreko
describes commissioning with two diesel generators and a battery, reported as
3 MW plus 1 MW BESS, with advanced controls.[2] The supplier also describes
the package/base case as 4.5 MW. Those figures are not fully reconciled; none
should be presented as independently measured peak demand.

This is evidence of a low-single-digit-MW commissioning package at a much
larger facility. It does not prove every turbine was tested at full power
simultaneously or that Aura units can directly replace the system. Gas supply,
voltage, transient response, battery integration and grid-emulation controls
require confirmation. Bare-generator rental and turnkey commissioning are
different scopes. The project's completed status rules it out as a live lead.

### Pipeline testing, Texas

Aggreko reports a 1 MW generator powering a chiller during pipeline testing,
alongside drying equipment.[3] This supports equipment-specific commissioning
demand rather than percentage-based sizing. Generator fuel is not established
by the cited account; a diesel air compressor does not prove generator fuel.
This is a completed application example, not a current solicitation.

### Data-center bridge power, Dublin

Aggreko describes 14 MW from twelve gas generators, distribution equipment
and 1 MW battery storage while grid service was delayed.[4] This supports
modular gas bridge power at substantial scale, not a small commissioning-load
ratio. It is a completed non-US case and establishes neither US approvals nor
Aura compatibility. Planned bridge operation and commissioning/test power
should be searched and qualified separately.

## Qualification requirements

| Gate | Required evidence | Without evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary scope | Tender, EPC package or owner statement | Research candidate only |
| Load and duty | Peak/continuous kW, kVA, starting currents, load steps, runtime and phases | MW and fleet count UNKNOWN |
| Timing | On-site date, test sequence, energization date, off-hire trigger | Timing UNKNOWN |
| Fuel | Fuel available during rental; flow, pressure, composition, logistics | Fuel fit UNKNOWN |
| Integration | Voltage, protection, island/grid operation, transformers, controls | Engineering fit UNKNOWN |
| Test requirements | Grid emulation, reverse-power handling, BESS/load banks where applicable | No turnkey equivalence claim |
| Site | Access, footprint, noise/emissions obligations and approvals | Delivery feasibility UNKNOWN |
| Buyer | Owner/EPC, current procurement, package boundaries, entry requirements | Not bid-ready |

Prioritize planned rental work compatible with available gas or propane
equipment. Emergency response and mixed-fleet maintenance remain excluded.
Planned operating support should be assessed on its actual obligations rather
than automatically equated with emergency response.

## Sizing discipline

For a documented 3,000 kW requirement, six nominal 500 kW units or ten nominal
310 kW units meet nameplate arithmetic. This is not engineered fleet sizing.
Use verified site-deliverable output, include auxiliaries and transient loads,
and test remaining capacity against required demand after the specified
contingency. An extra generator alone may not solve starting-current or
control-system requirements.

The supplied workbook contains cached Aura 408 outputs although its filename
references Aura 412. Formulas, revision applicability and operating assumptions
have not been validated. Do not quote its example as an approved derating
curve. NG/propane flexibility likewise does not prove liquid-fuel backup or
seamless transfer compliance.
