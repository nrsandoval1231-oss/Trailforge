## Product envelope

- Aura 408 R: 310 kW natural gas / 260 kW propane.
- Aura 412 R: 500 kW natural gas / 400 kW propane.
- Fuel-flexibility claims include lower-methane gas, LPG, and wellhead gas,
  with limited derating; hydrogen capability is described as proven technology.
- Product materials describe EPA certification, ultra-low NOx rich-burn
  technology with an onboard three-way catalyst, I.R.I.S. predictive
  maintenance and remote monitoring, and plug-and-play design.
- The workbook demonstrates that usable output is site-condition dependent.
  Its example for an Aura 408 at 875 m altitude, 45 C, and MTZ 80 calculates
  approximately 246 kW electrical output and approximately 222 kW in island
  operation. These are scenario calculations, not universal nameplate claims.

## Preferred RFP patterns

Prioritize solicitations for natural-gas or dual-fuel prime/bridge power,
temporary generation at sites with gas access, data-center or critical-load
resilience, microgrids, distributed generation, and projects seeking lower
emissions or fuel flexibility. Favor requirements in the 260-500 kW per-unit
range or scalable multi-unit deployments.

## Exclusion and downgrade rules

Downgrade or exclude generic diesel-fleet maintenance, broad generator repair
contracts, rental-only requirements with no natural-gas use case, and projects
whose response obligation is emergency field service rather than equipment
provision. Do not infer gas availability, connection feasibility, output after
derating, or rental demand from a project title.

## Negative example

The Morgan Hill generator maintenance RFP is not aligned with this ICP: it is a
30-unit mixed-fleet service contract, rental is incidental, the listed fleet
is mostly smaller diesel-style standby equipment, and the solicitation
requires emergency maintenance coverage. Retain it as a negative training
example, not as an active 2G opportunity.
