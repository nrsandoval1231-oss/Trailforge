## Product envelope

- Aura 408 R: 310 kW natural gas / 260 kW propane.
- Aura 412 R: 500 kW natural gas / 400 kW propane.
- Fuel-flexibility claims include lower-methane gas, LPG, and wellhead gas,
  with limited derating; hydrogen capability is described as proven technology.
- Product materials describe EPA certification, ultra-low NOx rich-burn
  technology with an onboard three-way catalyst, I.R.I.S. predictive
  maintenance and remote monitoring, and plug-and-play design.
- The workbook's cached example for an Aura 408 at 875 m altitude, 45 C, and MTZ 80 shows
  approximately 246 kW electrical output and approximately 222 kW in island
  operation. Formulas and model applicability have not been validated; these
  cached values are not approved sizing outputs or universal nameplate claims.

## Preferred RFP patterns

Prioritize solicitations for natural-gas or compatible propane prime/bridge power,
temporary generation at sites with gas access, data-center or critical-load
resilience, microgrids, distributed generation, and projects seeking lower
emissions or fuel flexibility. Favor requirements in the 260-500 kW per-unit
range or scalable multi-unit deployments. Large permanent-generation or
industrial-construction projects remain in scope when they separately need
temporary construction or commissioning power. A 300 MW facility with a
project-stated 3 MW commissioning requirement would require six 500 kW Aura
412 units or ten 310 kW Aura 408 units on nameplate arithmetic alone, before
allowing for load profile, derating, redundancy, and
interconnection constraints. Do not infer the 3 MW requirement from the 300 MW
nameplate; it must be stated or evidenced in the project documents.

Natural-gas/propane flexibility does not establish compliance with liquid-fuel
backup or automatic fuel transfer requirements. Confirm exact fuel, duty
rating, load-step capability and controls with 2G before qualification.

## Exclusion and downgrade rules

Downgrade or exclude generic diesel-fleet maintenance, broad generator repair
contracts, fuel requirements incompatible with the offered equipment, and projects
whose response obligation is emergency field service rather than equipment
provision. Do not infer gas availability, connection feasibility, output after
derating, commissioning load, or rental demand from a project title. Treat the
3 MW example as a planning estimate, not evidence, unless the RFP states it.

## Negative example

The Morgan Hill generator maintenance RFP is not aligned with this ICP: it is a
30-unit mixed-fleet service contract, rental is incidental, the listed fleet
is mostly smaller diesel-style standby equipment, and the solicitation
requires emergency maintenance coverage. Retain it as a negative training
example, not as an active 2G opportunity.
