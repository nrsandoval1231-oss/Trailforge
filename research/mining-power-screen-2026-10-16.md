## Stibnite: the conversation to have first

**Specific hypothesis:** if the camp is required for occupancy before sufficient permanent electrical service is accepted, its commissioning and interim operation may need a separately procured temporary or backup power package. This hypothesis is falsified if utility service meets the camp schedule and load, or if ATCO/Perpetua already has an adequate contracted supply.

**Why now:** Perpetua's 28 September 2026 release reports camp foundations/installation underway and ongoing powerline procurement. ATCO's 27 October 2025 announcement targeted camp operation in Q1 2027; that target is not a newly confirmed October 2026 handover date. Perpetua's public site shows current construction and access notices dated 12 October 2026.

**Buyer map:**

- Owner: Perpetua Resources.
- Procurement manager for major equipment/systems: Hatch, selected December 2025.
- Camp design/supply/installation contractor: ATCO Structures & Logistics (USA), already awarded.
- Potential project sponsor: Michael Wright, identified as VP Projects in ATCO's October 2025 announcement. Confirm current role; do not assume he personally purchases generators.
- Entry route: Perpetua's [vendor-intake page](https://perpetuaresources.com/contact/vendor-intake-form/). It explicitly accommodates electrical equipment and asks about Idaho presence. Registration does not establish qualification or access to a live bid. Use truthful supplier details and an Idaho service/logistics plan.

**The first question:**

> Who owns the Stibnite worker-housing electrical supply package—Perpetua/Hatch or ATCO—and is any commissioning, temporary, or backup generation scope still uncommitted ahead of the camp's current occupancy date? If so, what are the accepted utility energization date, phase-specific normal/peak kW, permitted fuels and supply logistics, operating duration, voltage/frequency, redundancy requirement, and bid process?

**2G-specific decision:** do not quote equipment yet. Verify gas/propane acceptability and delivered-fuel feasibility first. The sources reviewed do not establish natural-gas access, propane acceptance, load size, emissions approval, or service coverage. If diesel-only, pursue through a diesel-capable supplier, not a forced 2G substitution. If a hybrid rental arrangement is allowed, assess an integrator after the operating profile is known. If utility power is adequate, close the temporary-power hypothesis.

**Known exclusions:** ATCO's awarded housing contract and Idaho Power's contracted powerline materials are not available opportunities. A housing award does not establish that generator supply is included—or excluded. The requested conversation resolves that boundary.

## Important findings that prevented false positives

**Hermosa:** the 2024 ADEQ document describes alternative natural-gas engine fleets, 58+1 × 2,600 kW or 27+1 × 4,481 kW. Those are alternative permit configurations, not measured load or a current purchase quantity. The separate diesel scopes are not interchangeable with that gas scenario. South32's April 2026 announcement reports temporary self-generated gas power already in use. Its 14 September presentation says Notice to Proceed was received on 2 September and the 138 kV substation construction is complete, with federal-land line construction a FY27 milestone. The utility page's earlier expectation of September 2 approval is therefore superseded for the approval question; no grid energization date was established. South32 says it generally does not accept unsolicited supplier applications: follow relevant published opportunities or established contractor channels. No new power RFQ was found in this review.

**Rhyolite Ridge:** the August newsletter's end-September partnering target is a forecast, not proof that FID occurred. Its discussion of battery storage is general educational material and was not treated as a project BESS procurement requirement. Fluor's older DFS description and ABB's announced award need to be checked against the Hyundai procurement discussions before assuming current scope ownership.

**Thacker Pass:** the February forecast for powerline commissioning was replaced by the August report that off-site work was complete and energization remained targeted for Q4. Neither forecast establishes a delay or temporary MW requirement. Steam generation and grid supply already form the permanent-power plan.

**Eskay Creek:** September photographs and the August 31 progress statement support active construction, but do not prove current energized status or a power shortfall. March procurement statistics remain dated observations, not current unawarded-package percentages.
